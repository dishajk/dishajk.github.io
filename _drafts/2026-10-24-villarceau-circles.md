A few weeks ago a friend showed me this sliceform torus and asked, "how many circles can you draw on the surface of a torus that goes through a fixed point on the torus?"

There are two very familiar ones. One is the circle that lies on the plane parallel to the equatorial plane of the torus. The second is the one that lies on a plane perpedicular to the equatorial plane or in other words a plane that has the point and the axis of the torus.

If you go back and take another look at the sliceform paper construction model of the torus, you will notice that each of the cut papers are sections of a circle. 

Last year during, another friend brought my attention to the George Hart's paper construction models of polyhedrons made using regular polygons[^2].
